# Rama's 14-Year Exile on a Map | Ayodhya to Lanka, Explained

## Script

**1.** Rama walked from Ayodhya to Lanka. Plot the places that tradition links to his exile, and the route crosses most of the Indian subcontinent.
> On screen: AYODHYA → LANKA
> Visual: `geo`

**2.** One thing first. Valmiki names these places. He doesn't give map coordinates. The modern sites you'll see are traditional identifications, believed by pilgrims for centuries, not archaeological proof.
> On screen: TRADITION, NOT GPS
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**3.** It starts in Ayodhya, capital of the kingdom of Kosala. King Dasharatha has three queens and four sons. Rama is the eldest, and the king decides to crown him.
> On screen: AYODHYA
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile_of_Rama

**4.** But years earlier, Queen Kaikeyi had saved Dasharatha's life in battle, and he promised her two boons. A maid named Manthara convinces her to cash them in.
> On screen: TWO BOONS
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-ramayana-of-valmiki/d/doc423965.html

**5.** Boon one: her son Bharata gets the throne. Boon two: Rama goes into exile, in the Dandaka forest, for fourteen years.
> On screen: 14 YEARS
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-ramayana-of-valmiki/d/doc423965.html

**6.** Rama accepts without protest. A king's word must be kept. His wife Sita and his brother Lakshmana refuse to stay behind.
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile_of_Rama

**7.** Stop two. Shringverpur, on the Ganga. Here the boatman-king Guha ferries them across the river.
> On screen: SHRINGVERPUR
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**8.** Then Prayagraj, where the Ganga and Yamuna meet, and the hermitage of the sage Bharadvaja.
> On screen: PRAYAGRAJ
> Visual: `geo`

**9.** Stop four. Chitrakoot. Their first real forest home.
> On screen: CHITRAKOOT
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**10.** Back in Ayodhya, Dasharatha dies of grief. Bharata, horrified at what his mother has done, walks to Chitrakoot to bring Rama home.
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exile_of_Rama

**11.** Rama refuses. Fourteen years means fourteen years. So Bharata takes Rama's sandals, places them on the throne, and rules as a caretaker.
> On screen: THE SANDALS
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://sanskritdocuments.org/sites/valmikiramayan/ayodhya/sarga107/ayodhya_107_prose.htm

**12.** Then they go deep south, into Dandakaranya, a vast forest that tradition places across central India, including the Bastar region of today's Chhattisgarh.
> On screen: DANDAKARANYA
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Van_Gaman_Path

**13.** Stop six. Panchavati, on the Godavari, at today's Nashik. The name means five banyan trees. This is where the story turns.
> On screen: PANCHAVATI
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**14.** Here, Ravana's sister Surpanakha meets the brothers and is spurned. Lakshmana cuts off her nose, and she flies to Lanka for revenge.
> On screen: SURPANAKHA
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana

**15.** A golden deer appears. Rama chases it. Lakshmana follows. And Ravana, king of Lanka, carries Sita away.
> On screen: SITA IS TAKEN
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**16.** The old vulture Jatayu tries to stop him and falls. Tradition places that fight at Lepakshi, in today's Andhra Pradesh.
> On screen: LEPAKSHI
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**17.** Searching south, the brothers reach Kishkindha, the kingdom of the vanaras. It's usually identified with the area around Hampi, on the Tungabhadra river.
> On screen: KISHKINDHA · HAMPI
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/amp/story/experiences/heritage/ram-navami-footsteps-of-ram-sita-and-lakshman

**18.** Earlier, deep in the forest, an old woman named Shabari, who had waited years for Rama, had welcomed them and pointed them toward Sugriva.
> On screen: SHABARI
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana

**19.** Sugriva is a monkey prince in hiding, driven out by his brother Vali. Rama kills Vali, and Sugriva becomes king. In return, he sends his search parties out in every direction.
> On screen: SUGRIVA · VALI
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana

**20.** Sugriva's minister Hanuman crosses the sea, finds Sita in Lanka, shows her Rama's ring, and brings back her jewel as proof.
> On screen: HANUMAN
> Visual: `geo`

**21.** Before he leaves, Hanuman lets himself be captured. His tail is set on fire, and he uses it to burn Lanka.
> On screen: LANKA BURNS
> Visual: `kinetic`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana

**22.** Ravana's own brother, Vibhishana, begs him to return Sita. Ravana throws him out, and Vibhishana crosses over to Rama's side.
> On screen: VIBHISHANA
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana

**23.** The army marches to the coast at Rameswaram, the last stop in India.
> On screen: RAMESWARAM
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**24.** In the epic, the vanaras build a bridge across the sea. Today, between Rameswaram's island and Sri Lanka's Mannar Island, there's a chain of limestone shoals about forty eight kilometres long, called Ram Setu, or Adam's Bridge.
> On screen: RAM SETU · 48 KM
> Visual: `geo`
> Source: https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2024/06/Earth_from_Space_Adam_s_Bridge

**25.** Geologists describe the shoals as natural, a former land link exposed and drowned as sea levels changed. For millions of devotees, it is Rama's bridge. You can hold both facts at once.
> Visual: `cards`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam%27s_Bridge

**26.** The war is fought in Lanka. Ravana falls. Sita is free. And fourteen years after they left, they fly home to Ayodhya.
> Visual: `geo`

**27.** Add up only the straight lines between these stops, and it's already about three and a half thousand kilometres. On foot, through forest, it would be far more.
> On screen: ~3,600 KM
> Visual: `number`
> Source: https://hindutone.com/temples/ramayana-places-in-india-trail/

**28.** Today, governments are turning this into a pilgrimage circuit, the Ram Van Gaman Path. Two hundred and forty eight places in India and Sri Lanka have been identified along it.
> On screen: 248 PLACES
> Visual: `number`
> Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Van_Gaman_Path
