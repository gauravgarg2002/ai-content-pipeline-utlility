## Script

**1.** The greatest war in Indian myth was fought between cousins. Their great-uncle commanded one army. And the man who wrote the whole story was grandfather to both sides. To see how, you need one family tree.

**2.** One note before we start. The Mahabharata exists in many versions and retellings. This video follows Vyasa's Sanskrit epic, the first book called the Adi Parva, as translated into English by Kisari Mohan Ganguli in the 1880s and 90s.
> On screen: VERSION: VYASA · ADI PARVA · GANGULI
> Visual: `kinetic_text`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**3.** It begins with Shantanu, king of Hastinapura, of the Kuru dynasty. Shantanu marries Ganga, the river goddess, on one condition: he must never question anything she does.
> On screen: SHANTANU + GANGA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**4.** Ganga gives birth to seven sons and returns each one to the river. When she carries the eighth to the water, Shantanu finally stops her. Ganga leaves, but the eighth son lives. His name is Devavrata.
> On screen: THE EIGHTH SON: DEVAVRATA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**5.** Years later, Shantanu falls for Satyavati, a fisherman's daughter. Her father agrees to the marriage only if Satyavati's son becomes king. But the heir is Devavrata.
> On screen: SATYAVATI
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**6.** So Devavrata makes two vows. He gives up the throne, and he swears never to marry, so no child of his can ever claim it. The vow is so terrible that from then on he's called Bhishma, the one of the terrible oath. His father grants him a boon: he will die only when he chooses to.
> On screen: THE TERRIBLE VOW → BHISHMA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**7.** Now here's a detail that changes everything later. Before her marriage, Satyavati already had a son, born to the sage Parashara. That son became a great sage himself. His name is Vyasa. Remember him.
> On screen: SATYAVATI + PARASHARA → VYASA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**8.** Shantanu and Satyavati have two sons, Chitrangada and Vichitravirya. Chitrangada dies young in battle. Vichitravirya becomes king, and Bhishma goes to find him wives.
> On screen: CHITRANGADA · VICHITRAVIRYA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**9.** Bhishma carries off three princesses of Kashi: Amba, Ambika and Ambalika. Amba is in love with another king, so she is let go. Her story comes back for Bhishma, but that's another episode. Ambika and Ambalika marry Vichitravirya.
> On screen: AMBA · AMBIKA · AMBALIKA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**10.** Then Vichitravirya dies, with no children. The Kuru line is about to end. Bhishma is bound by his vow and refuses to father an heir. So Satyavati turns to her other son, Vyasa.
> On screen: NO HEIR
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**11.** Through an ancient custom called niyoga, Vyasa fathers children with the two widowed queens. Ambika shuts her eyes in fear, and her son Dhritarashtra is born blind. Ambalika turns pale, and her son Pandu is born pale. A maid of the palace also bears Vyasa a son, the wise Vidura.
> On screen: DHRITARASHTRA · PANDU · VIDURA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**12.** So the three brothers at the centre of the story aren't Shantanu's grandsons by blood at all. They're Vyasa's sons. Hold on to that.
> On screen: ALL THREE: SONS OF VYASA
> Visual: `family_tree`

**13.** Because Dhritarashtra is blind, Pandu is crowned king. Dhritarashtra marries Gandhari, and they have a hundred sons and one daughter, Duhshala. The eldest son is Duryodhana. These hundred are the Kauravas.
> On screen: 100 SONS → THE KAURAVAS
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**14.** Two details about this branch matter later. When Gandhari learns her husband is blind, she blindfolds her own eyes and keeps them covered for life. And Dhritarashtra has one more son, Yuyutsu, born to a maid. When the war comes, Yuyutsu is the one Kaurava who crosses over to fight for the other side.
> On screen: GANDHARI'S BLINDFOLD · YUYUTSU
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**15.** Pandu marries two queens, Kunti and Madri. Then, while hunting, he shoots a deer that turns out to be a sage named Kindama, in the form of a deer. The dying sage curses him: if Pandu ever lies with a wife, he will die. So Pandu cannot father children. Kunti has a secret gift. As a girl, the sage Durvasa gave her a mantra to call any god and bear his child.
> On screen: KUNTI · MADRI · THE MANTRA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**16.** Kunti calls on Dharma and bears Yudhishthira. On Vayu, the wind, and bears Bhima. On Indra, and bears Arjuna. She shares the mantra with Madri, who calls the twin Ashvins and bears the twins Nakula and Sahadeva. These five are the Pandavas.
> On screen: THE FIVE PANDAVAS
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**17.** The curse comes true. Years later, in the forest, Pandu forgets himself with Madri and dies. Madri follows him onto the funeral pyre, and Kunti brings all five boys back to Hastinapura, to grow up alongside their hundred cousins.
> On screen: PANDU DIES · BACK TO HASTINAPURA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**18.** But Kunti had used the mantra once before, as an unmarried girl. She called the sun god Surya, and bore a son, Karna. Afraid, she set the baby afloat on a river. He was raised by a charioteer. Karna is Kunti's first-born, the eldest Pandava, and he will fight for the Kauravas.
> On screen: KARNA: THE HIDDEN FIRST SON
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**19.** The charioteer is Adhiratha, and his wife Radha raises the boy as her own. That's why Karna is also called Radheya, son of Radha. He grows up believing he is a charioteer's son, and Duryodhana becomes his closest friend.
> On screen: ADHIRATHA + RADHA → RADHEYA
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**20.** One more link. Kunti's brother is Vasudeva, the father of Krishna. So Krishna, the Pandavas' guide in the war, is their first cousin.
> On screen: KRISHNA: COUSIN
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**21.** And the last name on the tree before the war: Draupadi, daughter of King Drupada of Panchala. Arjuna wins her in a contest of archery, and she becomes the wife of all five Pandava brothers.
> On screen: DRAUPADI
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**22.** Now step back and look at the whole tree. The Pandavas and the Kauravas are first cousins, sons of two brothers. Bhishma, who leads the Kaurava army, is great-uncle to both sides. Karna fights against his own half-brothers without knowing it for most of his life.
> On screen: COUSINS · GREAT-UNCLE · BROTHERS
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**23.** And at the very top of the living family sits Vyasa. Grandfather of the Pandavas. Grandfather of the Kauravas. And, according to tradition, the author of the Mahabharata itself.
> On screen: VYASA: GRANDFATHER · AUTHOR
> Visual: `family_tree`
> Source: https://sacred-texts.com/hin/maha/index.htm

**24.** So when the war at Kurukshetra begins, it isn't two kingdoms at war. It's one family. And the man telling you the story is watching his own grandsons fight.
> On screen: ONE FAMILY. ONE WAR.
